Lumina

A 3D color sphere for studying how light, shadow and base color interact, and for picking colors out of your artwork.

The sphere's colors change only from the sliders, the three target dots, or the eyedropper. Clicking the sphere does not change the sphere.

The panel

  • Header row. Left block holds the original color — click it to revert the active color back to it. Right block holds the active color — click it to commit it as the new original. The eyedropper hands over to Krita's Color Sampler so you can sample any pixel on the canvas.
  • The orb. A sphere lit by the three target colors. The small ring that follows your pointer is the color sampler; its center shows the exact color being sampled and the value appears underneath.
  • Target dots. Pick which color the Hue and Saturation sliders edit — moon for shadow, droplet for base, sun for light. The selected dot has a light ring around it.
  • Hue / Saturation / Contrast. Hue and Saturation edit the selected target. Contrast controls how sharply the light falls off into shadow; it does not change a color.
  • Advanced. A collapsible group holding Base Level, Intensity, Ambient, Specular, Glow, Tone and the light mixer. The panel scrolls when it does not fit.
  • Bottom row. Artistic for bright white highlights, Real for warm natural ones, and Apply to send the base color to Krita's foreground.

Picking a color

Click or drag on the sphere to choose that color for drawing. This sets Krita's foreground (brush) color so you can paint with it; the sphere itself is unchanged. Picks only register on the sphere — the corners outside the disc are ignored. Just past the silhouette a pick glides around the rim instead of stopping dead, so colors at the very edge stay reachable.

Press the eyedropper, then click a pixel on the canvas, to adopt a color from your artwork. The sampled color becomes the base and the rest of the lighting is derived around it, so the three always blend. This follows the usual hue-shifting rule: the light closes 45% of the distance toward white, desaturates, and moves its hue 35% of the way toward the warm key light; the shadow drops to 42% of the base's value, mutes slightly, and moves its hue 30% of the way toward the cool ambient — the complement of that light. Your previous tool is restored and the eyedropper stays armed.

Settings (gear)

Light azimuth and height rotate and raise the light, Quality sets the render resolution, Show color sampler toggles the pointer ring, and Reset all restores every default.

Save settings writes your setup to a plain-text file. Changes already save as you make them, so this is a checkpoint rather than the only chance to keep your work. The three target colors, light direction, quality, sampler visibility and every slider are restored next time Krita opens. The color you last picked for painting is not saved.

Tips

  • Shadows are rarely black. Give the shadow a real color — a cool bounce from the environment — rather than letting it go to black.
  • The base color is independent of the light and shadow, so you can restyle an object without disturbing its lighting.
  • Use the left block to A/B: pick a color, edit it, click the left block to go back, then the right block to keep the new one.
  • Drop Quality to Low on large documents if adjusting a slider feels laggy.
